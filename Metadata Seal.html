    How the seal works

    1. The file is split into its parts. Everything the picture does not need is pulled out: Exif and GPS, XMP, IPTC, comments, embedded thumbnails, and anything stored after the image.
    2. Those parts are encrypted. Your passphrase runs through 600,000 rounds of PBKDF2-SHA256 with a fresh random salt, and the resulting key encrypts the parts with AES-256-GCM.
    3. The ciphertext goes back into the file as one private block that photo viewers skip. The compressed picture data is copied as it is.
    4. Unsealing puts every part back where it was and compares the result with a SHA-256 of the original, so you know the file is identical.

    What it does not do

    • It does not change the picture.A street sign or house number in the frame is still in the frame.
    • It does not hide that a seal exists.Anyone can see an encrypted block and its size, rounded up to the next kilobyte.
    • It does not survive re-encoding.Social networks and most messaging apps rebuild photos on upload and drop the block. Send sealed photos as files: email attachment, shared drive, USB.
    • It cannot recover a lost passphrase.The photo still opens. The metadata stays locked.
    • It does not read HEIC, RAW or video.JPEG and PNG only.
